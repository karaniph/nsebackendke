# Treasury bonds for beginners - 2026-05-19

## Concept
Treasury bonds are long-term government securities designed for investors who want steadier income and lower credit risk than ordinary shares. The main building blocks are the coupon, the maturity date, and the yield. Once you understand those three, you can make better decisions about whether a bond belongs in your portfolio.

The most important point for beginners is that bonds have interest-rate risk. A bond is not the same thing as cash. If yields in the market rise after you buy, the resale value of your bond can fall. If yields decline, the bond can rise in value. That is why bond investing is not only about clipping coupons. It is also about timing, duration, and reinvestment discipline.

## NSE Context
In Kenya, bond education becomes more useful when the equity tape is not giving investors a strong broad-market signal. Around this session, market participation was active, but official index-level detail was thin. In that kind of environment, fixed-income benchmarks help investors avoid forcing risk into uncertain equity setups.

CBK policy, T-bill yields, and overall liquidity conditions matter because they influence how much return investors can demand from both equities and bonds. If short-term yields remain attractive, the pressure rises on expensive stocks to justify their valuations through real earnings delivery rather than narrative alone.

## Practical Example
Suppose you are deciding between parking funds in a long treasury bond or staying fully exposed to equities. If you expect rates to stay firm, you should be careful about buying long duration at the wrong level because price sensitivity will be higher. But if your goal is income stability and capital preservation, a bond may still be the cleaner choice than chasing thin market momentum.

A beginner should also compare laddering options. Instead of putting everything into one long-dated paper, you can spread exposure across maturities. That reduces reinvestment pressure and avoids anchoring the entire decision to one interest-rate view.

## Common Mistakes
- Treating treasury bonds as risk-free in every sense instead of recognizing price sensitivity to yield changes.
- Ignoring duration and buying long maturities simply because the coupon looks attractive.
- Using bonds as a substitute for portfolio planning instead of as one tool inside a broader allocation strategy.

## Checklist
- Define whether the goal is income, liquidity management, capital preservation, or tactical duration exposure.
- Compare bond yields with inflation, T-bill alternatives, and expected equity returns.
- Review CBK policy direction and your own time horizon before increasing long-duration exposure.
