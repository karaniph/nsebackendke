## Why this matters
Stock market lingo every NSE investor should know is one of the building blocks of disciplined investing on the Nairobi Securities Exchange. Getting it right helps you size positions sensibly, judge value, and avoid decisions driven by headlines. Cover practical definitions: P/E, EPS, dividend yield, payout ratio, market cap, and ROE.

## The concept
Treat this as a tool for organising judgement rather than a standalone buy or sell signal. The useful question is always whether the current facts support the decision, whether the risk is affordable, and whether the expected return beats the alternatives in cash, T-bills, bonds, or other listed equities.

## How it works on the NSE
Kenyan counters differ widely — a bank, a telco, a brewer, and a small illiquid stock cannot be judged the same way. Apply the concept with attention to sector, liquidity, and the prevailing rate environment set by the CBK.

## Worked example
- Write down your assumptions, then compare the position against a T-bill or money-market alternative before committing capital.

## Common mistakes
- Treating one metric as a complete investment thesis.
- Ignoring whether the latest disclosure is incomplete or low quality.
- Applying the same rule of thumb across banks, telecoms, consumer names, and thin small-caps.
- Confusing a good company with a good entry price.

## Your checklist
- Start from the latest filings and verified market context.
- Check that the approach fits the sector you are analysing.
- Pair it with risk, liquidity, and balance-sheet checks before acting.
- Compare the expected return with T-bills, bonds, and dividends.
- Revisit when rates, FX, earnings, or corporate actions change.
