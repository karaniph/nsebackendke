## Why this matters
These metrics turn a price quote into a picture of value and risk. They help you compare stocks quickly and avoid paying for hype. In practice, looking at P/E, EPS, dividend yield, payout ratio, market cap, and ROE together provides a more rounded view of how a company might perform relative to its price. The goal is to translate raw price information into indicators that reflect earnings and capital efficiency, so investment decisions can be based on relationships rather than on headlines alone. The material presented here is intended to illuminate how these inputs connect to a price list and to the earnings reports that feed them.

## The concept
P/E, price-to-earnings ratio, compares the price of a share to the earnings it generated. EPS, earnings per share, is the portion of profit allocated to each share. Dividend yield is the annual dividend per share as a percentage of the share price. Payout ratio is the portion of earnings paid out as dividends per share. Market cap is the total value of the company on the market, price per share times shares outstanding. ROE, return on equity, shows how efficiently a company uses shareholders’ capital by dividing net income by shareholder equity.

These definitions sit at the core of how investors interpret a stock’s price. The P/E ratio can be calculated using trailing twelve months earnings unless forward estimates are used. EPS comes from the earnings report; dividend yield depends on the declared dividend per share relative to the price. Market cap is simply price per share times the number of shares outstanding and is shown on price lists and stock screens. ROE is reported as part of the equity section of the financials; it gauges how much profit a firm earns on every shilling of equity. ## Worked example
- Assume a Kenyan counter called ABC Ltd with a closing price of 120 KES per share.
- Earnings per share (EPS) is 6 KES.
- The company pays an annual dividend per share of 5 KES.
- Shares outstanding are 200 million.
- Net income is 240 million KES and shareholder equity is 600 million KES. These figures are illustrative.
- P/E ratio equals price divided by EPS: 120 / 6 = 20x.
- Dividend yield equals dividend per share by price: 5 / 120 = 4.17%.
- Payout ratio equals dividends per share by earnings per share: 5 / 6 ≈ 83%.
- Market cap equals price times shares outstanding: 120 × 200,000,000 = 24,000,000,000 KES (KES 24 billion).
- ROE equals net income divided by shareholder equity: 240,000,000 / 600,000,000 = 40%.

## Common mistakes
- Relying on a single metric to judge a stock.
- Using trailing P/E when forward expectations are starkly different.
- Ignoring how high or rising debt affects true value.
- Mixing numbers from different currencies or share counts.
- Not checking whether EPS includes extraordinary items or discontinued operations.

These pitfalls can skew interpretation if you don’t consider the broader context. For example, a low P/E might reflect growth concerns rather than inexpensive valuation, and a high ROE by itself doesn’t reveal whether earnings growth is sustainable if equity is being used inefficiently. Cross-checking with the dividend history, payout behavior, and debt levels, as well as comparing against peers, helps mitigate these common missteps. Maintaining consistency in currency and share count across calculations is essential to avoid apples-to-oranges comparisons. The guidance here emphasizes careful attention to what is included in EPS and how it lines up with the reported earnings and equity sections in financial statements.

## Your checklist
- Verify whether EPS is trailing or forward and align with your analysis.
- Compare P/E and ROE against peers in the same sector.
- Check dividend history and whether the yield is sustainable.
- Look at the payout ratio in the context of growth and capex needs.
- Confirm market cap size relative to earnings and growth trajectory.

These checks are designed to keep valuation work grounded in the actual inputs that feed the metrics. By confirming the nature of EPS (trailing vs forward) and ensuring comparability with peers, you reduce the risk of misinterpreting the numbers after they come from the Equity Price List or the earnings reports. The emphasis on sustainability of dividends and the growth needs that drive payout decisions helps avoid overreliance on a single, potentially misleading, data point. The market cap comparison against earnings and growth also provides a pragmatic sense of scale and opportunity within a given sector.

## FAQ
- What does a low P/E tell me? It can indicate the price is cheap relative to earnings, but it may also reflect growth concerns or cyclicality.
- Why is ROE important for NSE stocks? It signals how efficiently a company uses equity to generate profits, a proxy for management effectiveness.
- Should I rely on one metric or several? Use a combination of P/E, EPS, dividend yield, payout, ROE, and debt levels to form a view.
