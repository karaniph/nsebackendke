## Market Pulse

The Nairobi Securities Exchange demonstrated steady liquidity conditions in the March 17, 2026 session, with aggregate turnover reaching KES 1.11 billion across 50.47 million shares traded. This level of market activity indicates sustained institutional participation despite the absence of foreign flow data, suggesting domestic investors are actively repositioning portfolios ahead of the first quarter earnings season. The NSE All Share Index (NASI) closed at 216.08 while the NSE 20 settled at 3,750.45, reflecting a market caught between optimism in consumer discretionary sectors and caution toward financial services exposed to asset quality deterioration. The turnover figure, while not exceptional by historical standards, represents sufficient depth to prevent volatile price swings in large-cap names, though the concentration of activity in mid-tier stocks suggests selective risk appetite rather than broad-based bullish sentiment.

## What Moved

### Top Gainers Signal Consumption Recovery

Unga Group Limited surged 9.75 percent to lead the gainers' board, followed closely by Uchumi Supermarkets rising 9.74 percent and HF Group advancing 6.67 percent. This clustering of consumer-facing and retail-oriented stocks at the top of the performance table suggests investors are pricing in a potential rebound in domestic consumption patterns, possibly driven by easing inflationary pressures or improved liquidity conditions in the agricultural value chain. For Unga specifically, the move likely reflects market positioning ahead of quarterly results that may benefit from stable wheat prices and improved distribution efficiencies, while Uchumi's rally could indicate speculative interest in turnaround stories within the distressed retail sector. HF Group's gain is particularly noteworthy given the property financier's exposure to Kenya's mortgage market, suggesting investors may be anticipating stabilization in real estate valuations or regulatory forbearance on non-performing property loans. The synchronized strength across these three diverse consumer proxies implies sector rotation toward domestic cyclicals and away from export-oriented or dollar-denominated revenue streams.

### Decliners Under Pressure

Eveready East Africa led the decliners, shedding 5.04 percent, followed by Nairobi Business Ventures dropping 3.90 percent and Carbacid Investments slipping 2.40 percent. Eveready's weakness likely stems from structural challenges in the dry-cell battery market as grid reliability improvements reduce household demand for backup power solutions, while competition from cheaper imports continues to erode market share. Nairobi Business Ventures, still recovering from its Deacons heritage, appears to be experiencing profit-taking following any recent speculative runs, with the retail sector remaining structurally challenged by high operating costs and shifting consumer preferences toward informal retail channels. Carbacid's modest decline may reflect industrial sector caution, as the carbon dioxide manufacturer serves the beverage and industrial gas markets that remain sensitive to manufacturing sector contraction indicators. The relatively modest percentage declines across the losers' list, compared to the sharp gains among top performers, suggests asymmetric risk appetite favoring upside accumulation over panic selling.

### Corporate Actions and Income Play

British American Tobacco Kenya continues to attract dividend-focused portfolios following its KES 60.00 final dividend declaration payable February 27, 2026. This substantial payout represents one of the highest absolute dividend yields on the exchange, providing defensive anchoring for income portfolios amid volatility in growth-oriented sectors. The attractiveness of this cash return becomes amplified when considering the limited alternative yield opportunities in the fixed income space, where T-bill yields remain subdued, though specific rates were not captured in the current reporting cycle.

## Sector Trends

### Banking Sector Dynamics

KCB Group remains the focal point for financial sector analysis, with the counter demonstrating the complex duality currently characterizing Kenyan banking stocks. The lender's FY24 and HY24 earnings reveal robust profit growth trajectory supported by strong non-interest income streams and regional expansion, yet this performance is increasingly threatened by rising non-performing loans and escalating funding costs. The divergence between top-line profitability and underlying asset quality creates a valuation tension for investors, as the bank trades at attractive earnings multiples but carries embedded credit risk that may not be fully priced into the current share valuation. Particularly concerning is the concentration of NPLs within the SME, agriculture, energy, and construction sectors, which together represent systemic vulnerabilities given Kenya's current macroeconomic headwinds including currency depreciation and elevated input costs.

The sector-wide margin compression stems from higher deposit attraction costs as banks compete for stable funding amid tight liquidity conditions. This funding cost pressure coincides with regulatory delays in the National Bank of Kenya sale, creating uncertainty regarding the ultimate resolution of distressed assets within the sector and potentially crowding out private sector credit extension as banks conserve capital for prospective consolidation activities. The technical insolvency risks in smaller tier-three institutions may also be driving flight-to-quality toward KCB and Equity Group, though even these larger franchises are not immune to the systemic NPL expansion.

### Industrial and Consumer Sentiment

The industrial sector shows bifurcated performance, with Carbacid's decline contrasting against Unga Group's strength, suggesting market participants are distinguishing between commodity-linked industrial plays and food security defensive positions. The milling sub-sector, represented by Unga, benefits from policy support regarding maize imports and wheat subsidies that insulate margins from global commodity volatility, while industrial gas manufacturers face pricing pressure from manufacturing sector demand contraction.

## Risks

### Asset Quality Deterioration

The most pressing systemic risk remains the trajectory of non-performing loans across the banking sector, particularly KCB Group's exposure to SME and agricultural lending. As regional currencies depreciate against the dollar, unhedged foreign currency liabilities in the corporate sector threaten to trigger covenant breaches and rollover difficulties, potentially converting performing loans into substandard credits. The construction and energy sectors face specific refinancing risks as project finance structures assume currency stability that East African markets have not provided consistently. Investors should monitor the Central Bank of Kenya's forthcoming credit surveys for confirmation of sector-wide NPL ratios exceeding historical averages.

### Funding and Liquidity Constraints

Higher funding costs represent a structural margin threat that may persist longer than equity markets currently anticipate. With deposit growth competing against informal savings mechanisms and regional currency instability discouraging dollarization, banks face a costly battle for liability stability that will compress net interest margins throughout 2026. The interbank rate, currently not disclosed in available data but historically volatile, serves as a barometer for systemic liquidity conditions that could force emergency liquidity assistance if stress intensifies.

### Regulatory Execution Risk

The prolonged delays in the National Bank of Kenya resolution create regulatory uncertainty that extends beyond the immediate parties involved. Stakeholder fatigue regarding the transaction timeline suggests potential haircuts or conversion scenarios that could establish precedent for future distressed bank resolutions, potentially spooking minority shareholders across the sector. Additionally, regulatory approaches to NPL recognition forbearance may mask true asset quality levels, creating opacity regarding the adequacy of loan loss provisions.

## What To Watch Next

Investors should monitor KCB Group's share price action for potential entry points, particularly if the counter corrects on NPL fears despite underlying earnings resilience, creating a valuation dislocation for long-term holders. The upcoming T-bill auctions will provide crucial signaling regarding monetary policy trajectory and government borrowing appetite, with any yield spikes potentially triggering rotation from equities to fixed income. Foreign investor flow data, currently unavailable but typically released with lag, will indicate whether the current turnover levels reflect domestic speculative activity or genuine offshore accumulation. Finally, quarterly results from the industrial sector, particularly Unga and Eveready, will test the consumption recovery thesis underlying recent price movements in the consumer discretionary space.
