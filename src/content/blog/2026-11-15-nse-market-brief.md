## Key takeaways
- Uchumi tops the list of movers, +6.40% to 1.33, with Eveready East Africa and ABSA not far behind, signaling targeted demand in consumer and financial names during a thin session.
- The day shows selective breadth: NASI up 0.08% to 241.74, NSE 20 up 0.34% to 4,188.86, and the Banking index up 0.50% to 284.07, while top loser QMRT slides about 7.19% to 8.26.
- Turnover and liquidity remain light by recent standards: turnover about 333.3 million Kenyan shillings, with net foreign inflows of 13.97 million and top inflows concentrated in Kenya Pipeline, Quickmart and KCB. Expect more volatility on skewed liquidity unless buyers return in force.

## Market pulse
The session painted a mixed but constructive tone for value names. NASI rose marginally, with breadth showing pockets of strength in banks and select retailers. NSE 20 ticked higher as risk appetite improved slightly, while the Bank Index advanced, underscoring ongoing rotation within financials rather than a broad market rally.
Turnover clocked in around 333.30 million KES, and volumes ran lighter than in the stronger weeks that preceded. Foreign flow remained positive, narrowing the gap between domestic and foreign participation. Net inflows of 13.97 million KES came despite some selling in defensive names, reflecting a cautious, but constructive, sentiment from offshore participants.
Top foreign buys focused on Kenya Pipeline Co., Quickmart, and KCB, while outbound only showed notable weakness in ABSA NewGold ETF and Safaricom, signaling selective preference rather than broad-based foreign demand.

## Sector & themes
- Banking and consumer names led the up-moves, while a handful of mid-cap retailers and utilities showed less robust performance. The Banking index rose 0.50%, confirming ongoing, modest rotation within the sector rather than a uniform rally.
- The retail and consumer space showed mixed signals, with Uchumi leading gains as QMRT weighed on the broader retail set. This points to selective appetite rather than broad-based demand, typical of a thin-session environment.
- Foreign flow remains a critical fulcrum for near-term moves. While the net inflow suggests overseas buyers are still net positive, the concentration of inflows to select names means price action can be choppy in days with liquidity gaps. The disappearance of broad, sustained buying pressure makes quick, tactical risk controls essential.
- The macro backdrop remains anchored by rates and FX, with domestic liquidity and event-driven catalysts driving intraday swings more than structural shifts in equity valuations.

## Risks
- Liquidity risk remains elevated in thin sessions; price gaps are plausible and can distort price discovery.
- A single large order in a thin name like QMRT or a key retailer could cause outsized moves in either direction.
- Persistent mixed foreign flows could cap upside unless more durable inflows resume, particularly into blue-chip banks and consumer staples.
- Any unexpected macro surprise or policy signal could reverse the current rotation, especially if it affects credit conditions or consumer sentiment.
