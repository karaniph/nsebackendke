What the concept means
Price-to-Book (P/B) compares a stock's market price to its book value per share (BVPS). For banks, BVPS reflects the accounting value of equity after provisions for loan losses and other asset quality adjustments. On the NSE, banks like KCB and EQTY are common references for P/B because their asset-heavy balance sheets are central to their accounting value. The metric helps identify situations where the market price may be undervalued or overvalued relative to the value on the books, especially when ROE and asset quality shift. In today’s context, the NSE’s broader backdrop includes the NSE20 at 3,997.59 and NASI at 209, alongside a 8.75% CBK rate, which influences investor sentiment around value plays like banks.

How it works on the NSE specifically
P/B is calculated as price per share divided by BVPS. For banks such as KCB and EQTY, BVPS is driven by tangible equity and an allowance for loan losses, making P/B more meaningful when asset quality is relatively stable. On the NSE, P/B is often used to screen for value opportunities in asset-heavy sectors where quick revaluation of intangible assets is limited. Investors typically pair P/B with ROE, earnings momentum, and loan-quality indicators to avoid misreading a bank’s market price in a stressed cycle. The current market environment—thin sessions, muted foreign flow signals, and a steady policy backdrop—can compress or expand P/B readings as capital markets reassess banks’ balance-sheet strength.

Common mistakes
Relying on P/B alone to judge a bank like KCB or EQTY; a high P/B may reflect strong ROE expectations rather than solid book-value support. 
Using understated BVPS when loan-loss provisions are rising; this can understate risk in banks with rising non-performing loan (NPL) ratios. 
Comparing P/B across sectors without adjusting for asset structure; asset-light firms will naturally show different baselines than asset-heavy banks. 
Ignoring the effect of dividends and capital adequacy on BVPS; share buybacks can compress BVPS even if market prices rise. 
Skipping liquidity context; in thin sessions like today, prices may drift and P/B signals may mislead if not paired with volume cues.
