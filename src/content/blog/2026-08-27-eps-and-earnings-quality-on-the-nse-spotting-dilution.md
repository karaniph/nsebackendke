Reading through EPS with a lens on quality helps you separate repeatable, cash-backed performance from quick-buck distortions that vanish when the market tests cash flow and payout sustainability.

## The concept
EPS stands for earnings per share. It’s calculated as net income divided by the number of shares outstanding. There’s also diluted EPS, which assumes all convertible securities and share options are exercised, increasing the share base and potentially depressing per-share profits. Earnings quality is the degree to which reported profits reflect recurring, cash-generating operations rather than one-offs, aggressive accounting, or capital-structure quirks.

Key ideas to grasp:
- Basic EPS versus diluted EPS: basic uses current shares; diluted accounts for potential extra shares. The latter gives a worst-case per-share view.
- One-off items: extraordinary gains or losses that aren’t expected to recur. They can lift or depress EPS without signaling ongoing profitability.
- Earnings quality vs headline EPS: a rising EPS that’s driven by share issuance or one-offs is not the same as growing, sustainable earnings supported by cash flow.

In the Kenyan market, where dividend announcements can influence investor sentiment, you’ll often see the payout policy reflect earnings quality. Payouts like the ones announced by SCOM and KNRE offer a practical link between earnings performance and cash returns to shareholders.

## How it works on the NSE
The NSE lists per-share metrics that investors frequently chase, but it’s crucial to read the notes behind the numbers. Here’s how EPS quality plays out in practice on Kenyan counters:
- Dilution risk: Rights issues, employee stock options, or convertible debt can saddle you with more shares. Dilution reduces diluted EPS, even if net income grows. When you compare EPS over quarters, look for both basic and diluted figures and check whether the company issued new shares or warrants.
- One-offs and accounting choices: A once-off gain or loss can distort EPS briefly. Compare core earnings (recurring profits) to reported earnings to assess how much of the EPS came from ongoing operations.
- Dividends as a quality signal: A sustainable payout ratio tends to accompany solid earnings quality. A company that pays a big dividend but shows weak recurring earnings warrants caution.
- Market context: In a thin-session environment like today’s, price moves can exaggerate or mask earnings signals. The NSE’s mix of movers (for instance, KNRE and SLAM) and the dividend cadence in KNRE/SCOM can influence how investors price earnings quality into stocks.

## Worked example
Think of a simple company, Zedco Ltd, as a realistic training example for how EPS and dilution interact. Use illustrative figures to keep it clear and practical.
- Baseline:
  - Net income: **KES 100 million** per year
  - Shares outstanding: **50 million**
  - Basic EPS: **KES 2.00** per share (100m / 50m)
- Dilutive scenario (rights issue): Zedco announces a rights issue for **10 million** new shares. Net income stays at 100m.
  - New shares: 60 million
  - Diluted EPS: 100m / 60m = **KES 1.67** per share
  - Reality check: the same earnings are now sliced across more shares, dragging EPS lower even if profits aren’t changing.
- Buyback scenario: Zedco buys back 10 million shares. Net income stays at 100m, but shares drop to 40 million.
  - Diluted and basic EPS converge in this example: 100m / 40m = **KES 2.50** per share
  - Why it matters: buybacks reduce the denominator, lifting EPS, but you should verify that the company is deploying capital efficiently rather than just boosting EPS numerically.
- One-off item: suppose a one-off gain adds **KES 20 million** to net income, boosting headline EPS without altering ongoing profitability.
  - If no new shares are issued, basic EPS becomes 120m / 50m = **KES 2.40**; diluted EPS becomes 120m / 60m = **KES 2.00** (assuming the 60m diluted shares). The key takeaway is to separate recurring earnings from one-offs when assessing quality.
- Core earnings test: if the recurring (operating) earnings are only growing alongside a one-off windfall, the stock’s true earning power may still be flat or deteriorating. In practice, compare core EPS (adjusted for one-offs) to reported EPS and see how much is truly repeatable.

This exercise shows three practical rules: (1) always check diluted EPS in addition to basic EPS, (2) watch for dilution events and their timing, and (3) treat one-offs as a separate line item when evaluating quality. On the NSE, where dividends and sudden price moves are common, this disciplined approach keeps you from overpaying for a temporary boost in earnings.

## Common mistakes
- Relying on headline EPS without checking dilution: basic EPS can look strong while diluted EPS deteriorates.
- Ignoring one-offs and accounting treatment: a spike from a one-off can mask weak recurring profits.
- Skipping cash-flow context: high EPS with negative operating cash flow is a red flag for quality.
- Overinterpreting dividend announcements without linking to earnings: sustainable dividends usually accompany solid, recurring earnings rather than episodic gains.
- Comparing EPS across periods without adjusting for share changes: new issuances or buybacks distort the comparison.
- Forgetting to read notes to accounts: many quality signals live in the footnotes, not the main earnings line.

## Your checklist
- Compare basic EPS and diluted EPS; note any dilution events or share issuances.
- Look for a stable to growing cash flow from operations; check the cash conversion of earnings.
- Review the payout ratio and dividend sustainability; ask whether earnings quality supports ongoing dividends.
- Separate recurring earnings from one-offs; assess core earnings growth.
- Read notes on accounting policies and one-time items; verify whether any off-balance-sheet items exist.
- Put earnings in context of market signals (e.g., liquidity, price action, dividend cadence) rather than in isolation.

## FAQ
- Q: What is the practical difference between basic EPS and diluted EPS?
  A: Basic EPS uses current shares outstanding; diluted EPS assumes all potential new shares from options, warrants, or convertibles are issued, often lower than basic EPS.
- Q: How can I tell if earnings are high quality?
  A: Look for strong recurring cash flow, a reasonable payout ratio, little reliance on one-offs, and minimal dilutive effects on EPS over time.
- Q: How do dividends relate to earnings quality on the NSE?
  A: Sustainable dividends typically reflect solid, recurring earnings and cash flow, whereas erratic or outsized dividends can mask weaker core profits.
