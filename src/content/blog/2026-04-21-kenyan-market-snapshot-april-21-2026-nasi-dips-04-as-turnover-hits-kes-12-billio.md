Key Takeaways
The NSE All Share Index (NASI) retreated 0.4% to 188.73, extending losses from the prior session as profit-taking in blue chips dominated trading.

Total market turnover declined to KES 1.2 billion, a 15% drop from Monday’s KES 1.4 billion, signaling a pullback in risk appetite among investors.

Safaricom led decliners with a 1.8% slide to KES 32.50, erasing KES 6.2 billion in market capitalization.

Manufacturing counters such as Bamburi Cement and Crown Berger advanced 2.1% to KES 285.00 and 1.5% to KES 124.50, respectively, underpinned by selective buying in industrials.

The shilling held firm at KES 132.45 per dollar, providing no immediate relief to importers facing elevated input costs.

Market Pulse
Equity markets opened on a cautious note, with the NASI slipping 0.2% to 189.12 at the opening bell before accelerating losses to 188.73 by midday. Turnover contracted to KES 1.2 billion, reflecting a broad-based reduction in activity after Monday’s KES 1.4 billion session.

Foreign investors remained net sellers, offloading KES 340 million in blue-chip counters, while domestic institutional buyers showed intermittent support in mid-cap names. The shilling’s stability at KES 132.45 against the dollar offered no respite to sectors reliant on imported raw materials, particularly manufacturing and energy.

The day’s trading pattern underscored a shift in investor preference toward defensive sectors, with industrials outperforming the broader market. Volume in industrials surged 22% above the 30-day average, driven by sustained demand in construction-linked stocks. Banking shares exhibited mixed performance, with Equity Group rising 0.3% to KES 42.00 on bargain hunting, while KCB Group fell 0.7% to KES 38.50 amid thin liquidity.

What Moved
Top Gainers
Bamburi Cement led advancers with a 2.1% gain to KES 285.00, extending its three-day rally as investors priced in resilient first-quarter volume growth. Trading volume surged 22% above the 30-day average.

Top Losers

Co-operative Bank declined 1.2% to KES 18.70, pressured by thin trading volumes and profit booking, contrasting with Equity Group’s modest gain.
