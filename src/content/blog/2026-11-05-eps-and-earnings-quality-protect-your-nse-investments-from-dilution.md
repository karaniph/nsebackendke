## Why this matters
EPS is a shorthand for profitability per share, but it can mislead if you ignore how it’s shaped by dilution and earnings quality. In a market like the NSE where liquidity can be light, a superficially strong EPS can mask weaker cash flows or equity dilutions that compress value for ordinary investors. The takeaway today is simple: focus on the quality of earnings and on whether the share count is changing when you see a headline EPS move.

## The concept
Earnings per share is defined as net income attributable to ordinary shareholders divided by the weighted average number of shares outstanding during the period. What matters is not just the headline number, but whether those earnings are sustainable and cash-generative. Dilution happens when a company issues new shares or when stock options, convertibles, or other instruments increase the share count. If earnings don’t grow in step with the higher share count, EPS falls even if total earnings are up.

Earnings quality is about how much of the reported earnings come from core, cash-generating operations versus accounting tricks, one-offs, or non-cash gains. High-quality earnings come with strong cash flows from operating activities and a clear link between net income and cash. Low-quality earnings can show as rising EPS but weak cash flow, or a surge from non-recurring items that isn’t repeatable.

## How it works on the NSE
On the Nairobi Securities Exchange, EPS is still the central yardstick investors use to value a company, but dilution risk and capital structure matter just as much as the earnings number itself. Watch for corporate actions that change the share count, such as rights issues, share placements, or employee stock options that convert into new shares. When such actions occur, the measured EPS can change even if net income is flat, because the denominator (shares outstanding) has grown.

Notes to keep in mind:
- Dilutive events can depress EPS even when the business is doing okay. Investors often see a temporary EPS dip after a rights issue or a new round of debt-to-equity conversion.
- Dilution is not inherently negative if the capital raised funds projects that lift future earnings meaningfully. The critical question is whether N' (post-dilution earnings) grows enough to offset the larger share base.
- In a market with light liquidity, misreading an EPS uptick caused by a one-off gain or a non-core item can misprice risk. Always cross-check earnings quality indicators and cash flow signals.

## Worked example
This is a clean, step-by-step framework you can apply to any NSE stock, without relying on any specific company numbers. Use it as a lens to judge whether an EPS move is sustainable and how dilution could be at play.

- Step 1: Define baseline metrics. Let N be net income attributable to ordinary shareholders for the period. Let S be the weighted average number of ordinary shares outstanding during the period. The basic EPS is EPS0 = N divided by S.
- Step 2: Account for a dilution event. Suppose the company issues new shares or their equivalents, increasing the share count to S1 = S plus ΔS, where ΔS captures the new shares issued or converted. If earnings do not change immediately, new EPS after the dilution is EPS1 = N divided by S1.
- Step 3: Consider earnings growth with dilution. If earnings grow to N1 after the dilution, the post-dilution EPS becomes EPS2 = N1 divided by S1. Compare EPS2 to EPS0 to judge whether the growth in earnings compensates for the higher share count.
- Step 4: Bring in earnings quality. Even if EPS2 looks higher, verify whether the uplift comes from core operating profits or from non-recurring items, accounting adjustments, or one-offs. Check cash flow from operating activities to see if cash profits align with the reported net income.
- Step 5: Apply a practical test. If you see EPS rising but operating cash flow lagging or if a large portion of earnings comes from a non-cash adjustment, treat the EPS improvement with caution. If the company has a history of dilutive issuances that precede capital projects, review the expected payback period and the quality of the cash flows those projects generate.

This framework keeps you focused on two things that matter in practice: is there dilution and, more importantly, is the earnings build sustainable in cash terms. When you see an upcoming corporate action, use the above steps to estimate how EPS might move and whether the implied price action is justified by cash-based profitability.

## Common mistakes
- Focusing on headline EPS without checking diluted EPS and capital structure changes.
- Ignoring the notes to accounts that discuss one-off gains or non-operating items that can inflate earnings temporarily.
- Assuming all earnings growth is sustainable without checking cash flow adequacy.
- Comparing companies with very different levels of leverage or with different rights issues and convertible securities as if the EPS numbers were apples to apples.
- Overreacting to a single day move in EPS while ignoring broader liquidity and macro signals from the market context, such as the CBK rate backdrop and debt-market signals.

## Your checklist
- Check whether diluted EPS is disclosed and compare it to basic EPS.
- Look for notes on new share issues, options, or convertible securities that could dilute earnings.
- Cross-check earnings with cash flow from operations to gauge quality.
- Compare EPS growth to revenue growth and to gross margins to test sustainability.
- Review the company’s track record on capital allocation and whether fresh equity funding funded productive assets.
- Consider the liquidity environment and whether a move in EPS aligns with broader market cues and debt-market signals.

## FAQ
- What is the difference between basic and diluted EPS?
  Basic EPS uses the current number of outstanding shares, while diluted EPS assumes all potential shares from options, convertibles, and other instruments are exercised or converted, showing the worst-case dilution effect.
- Why is earnings quality important for NSE investors?
  Because high reported EPS that rests on one-off gains, aggressive accruals, or weak cash flow may not translate into real, repeatable profits for shareholders.
- How can I spot dilution signals on the NSE quickly?
  Watch corporate action announcements for rights issues, share placements, or convertible debt; read the notes to the annual and quarterly reports for changes in the capital structure and for guidance on future earnings prospects.
