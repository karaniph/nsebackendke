Market Pulse

Market Overview
The Nairobi Securities Exchange (NSE) concluded trading on 24 July 2026 with modest gains, reflecting a session characterized by low volatility and thin liquidity. The benchmark NSE20 index closed at 3,997.50, marking a 0.34% increase from the previous session. This marginal uptick places the index just shy of the psychologically significant 4,000-point threshold, a level that market participants are closely monitoring for potential technical confirmation.

The NSE All Share Index (NASI) settled at 0.75, mirroring the subdued activity observed in the broader market. Equity turnover for the session reached KES 563.6 million, with a total of 15.04 million shares exchanging hands. While these figures indicate some level of trading activity, brokers described the session as "thin," suggesting that participation was limited and price movements were driven by a relatively small number of transactions.

Foreign investor flows remained unconfirmed at the time of reporting, adding an element of uncertainty to the session’s dynamics. The absence of clear foreign participation data complicates efforts to gauge the underlying sentiment driving the market’s modest gains. Brokers noted that without confirmation of inflows or outflows, it is difficult to determine whether the day’s price action was driven by domestic or international capital.

Top Movers
Intraday trading activity was concentrated around two of the market’s most liquid stocks: KCB Group (KCB) and Equity Group (EQTY). While specific price targets or volume figures were not disclosed in the available data, brokers highlighted these tickers as focal points for traders seeking liquidity in an otherwise quiet session. The emphasis on KCB and EQTY aligns with their historical status as bellwethers for the Kenyan banking sector, which often sets the tone for broader market movements.

The NSE20’s proximity to the 4,000-point level was a key technical consideration for market participants. Brokers advised that a decisive breakout above this threshold could serve as a confirmation signal, potentially attracting follow-through buying and reinforcing a short-term uptrend. Conversely, a rejection at this level might invite profit-taking or renewed selling pressure, particularly in the absence of strong volume or foreign participation. The lack of clarity around foreign flows further underscores the importance of monitoring this technical level, as external capital could play a decisive role in determining the market’s next directional move.

Risks
The thin liquidity observed during the session introduces several risks for market participants. Chief among these is the potential for whipsaw price action, where rapid and unpredictable reversals can lead to heightened volatility and increased trading costs. In such environments, even modest order sizes can have an outsized impact on prices, making it difficult for traders to execute strategies with precision.

The unconfirmed status of foreign flows adds another layer of risk. Foreign investor participation is a critical driver of liquidity and sentiment in the Kenyan market, and the lack of clarity around these flows complicates risk management. Brokers advised that position sizes should remain small until foreign flow data is verified, as sudden inflows or outflows could dramatically alter market dynamics.

Additionally, the AIB bond issuance filings indicate ongoing debt activity, though no specifics regarding tenor or yield were provided. The lack of details on this issuance introduces uncertainty, as the terms of the bond could influence market liquidity and investor appetite for risk assets. Until more information becomes available, market participants are likely to remain cautious, particularly in light of the broader macroeconomic environment.

Fixed Income & Macro
The Central Bank of Kenya (CBK) maintained its benchmark rate at 8.75% during its most recent policy meeting, reflecting a cautious stance amid ongoing economic uncertainties. The 91-day Treasury bill yield stood at 8.782%, slightly above the CBK’s policy rate, indicating a modest premium for short-term government debt. Data for longer-dated instruments, such as the 182-day and 364-day T-bills, was not available at the time of reporting.

The next Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) meeting is scheduled for 11 August 2026, where policymakers will assess economic conditions and determine whether further adjustments to the benchmark rate are warranted. Market participants will be closely watching this meeting for signals on the CBK’s inflation outlook and growth projections, as these factors could influence both equity and fixed income markets.

In the fixed income space, the AIB bond issuance filings suggest that debt activity is underway, though the lack of details on tenor or yield leaves many questions unanswered. The terms of this issuance could have implications for market liquidity and investor sentiment, particularly if the bond offers attractive yields relative to existing instruments. Until more information is disclosed, the fixed income market is likely to remain in a holding pattern, with participants awaiting clarity on both domestic and external factors.

Traders should stay alert to any announcements regarding this issuance, as it could serve as a catalyst for short-term market movements.
