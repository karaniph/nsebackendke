## Key Takeaways
- The NSE All Share Index (NASI) experienced a mixed session with low market turnover of KES 54 million.
- Equity Group (EQTY) was the session's most active counter, driving the bulk of trading activity.
- Centum Real Estate successfully retired its KES 3 billion green bond, reflecting improved corporate liquidity management.

## Market Pulse
The trading week commenced with a cautious tone as investors balanced the impact of corporate debt retirements against a backdrop of steady interest in the big banks. The retirement of the Centum green bond is a positive signal for the corporate bond market, potentially freeing up liquidity for other domestic investment avenues. Total session turnover of KES 54 million underscored the subdued participation levels typical of Monday sessions.

## What Moved
### Top Gainers
- Limuru Tea (LIMT) posted a modest gain as agricultural counters attracted selective interest from retail investors looking for value outside the banking sector.

### Top Losers
- Broader market laggards saw thin volume declines, with profit-taking weighing on select mid-cap counters that had rallied in prior sessions.

## Sector Trends
Financials dominated session activity, with Equity Group absorbing the lion's share of institutional flow. The banking sector continues to benefit from a favourable interest rate environment and strong earnings visibility heading into the next reporting cycle. Agricultural and manufacturing counters traded with minimal conviction, reflecting ongoing uncertainty around input costs and domestic demand conditions.

## Risks
**Liquidity Constraints** — Participation in smaller counters remains thin, amplifying price volatility on any sizable order flow. The KES 54 million turnover level signals a market operating well below its capacity, which could exacerbate price gaps on larger institutional orders.

**Corporate Debt Dynamics** — While the Centum bond retirement is positive, the broader corporate bond market remains under-developed, and any large-scale debt restructuring events could shift institutional attention away from equities.

## What To Watch Next
**Banking Earnings** — Monitor upcoming quarterly results from KCB and Co-operative Bank for confirmation of the sector's earnings trajectory.

**Fixed Income Competition** — Treasury bill auction results will signal whether institutions are rotating back into risk-free assets at the expense of equity allocations.

**Global Macro** — Track oil prices and USD/KES movements for directional cues on import-dependent sectors.
