## Why this matters
Inflation isn’t a trivia line in a macro report; it changes how much your wealth actually grows. For Kenyan retail investors on the NSE, real returns matter because money in your pocket has to stretch beyond price increases. Today’s market backdrop shows a thin session with NASI at **253.73** and NSE 20 at **4,451.17**, plus turnover around **KES 1.025b**—a reminder that what looks like a gain on paper can shrink after inflation and costs.

The big takeaway is simple: nominal gains are not the same as real gains. If inflation runs higher than your portfolio’s nominal return, your real return can be flat or even negative. This matters for retirement planning, education funding, and anything you’re building with long time horizons on the NSE.

## The concept
- Nominal return: the percentage price change of an asset (or a basket of assets) before adjusting for price increases.
- Inflation: the rate at which the general level of prices for goods and services rises, eroding purchasing power.
- Real return: what you actually earned after inflation. It’s essentially nominal return minus inflation.
- Real return example (in plain terms): if your fund earns a nominal return and inflation goes up, the real gain is the leftover after you account for higher prices. If inflation outpaces your gains, your real return goes negative.

Key formula: Real return ≈ Nominal return − Inflation. In practice, you compare real returns to your target purchasing-power growth, not just the headline percentage gain.

On the NSE, where indices like NASI and NSE 20 guide broad and blue-chip exposure, inflation shifts the interpretation of any move. Even a seemingly solid nominal gain can feel lackluster if inflation is high and your real purchasing power is shrinking. ## Worked example
- Step 1: Define the two numbers you care about for any asset: Nominal return (R_nom) and inflation (pi).
- Step 2: Compute real return using Real return ≈ R_nom − pi.
- Step 3: Compare two assets by their real returns rather than nominal gains. Asset A with R_nom_A and Asset B with R_nom_B will have Real_A ≈ R_nom_A − pi and Real_B ≈ R_nom_B − pi; whoever has the higher Real value wins in real terms.
- Step 4: Consider the impact of ongoing costs (fees, taxes, trading costs) that further reduce nominal gains. Those reductions occur before you subtract inflation in real terms.
- Step 5: Apply to a practical NSE-like scenario with a dividend payer vs a growth name. If inflation rises but both assets see nominal returns, the dividend payer’s cash payout can cushion real performance, but only if the payout keeps pace with or exceeds inflation in real terms.
- Step 6: Use the current context as a guide but rely on your own data: identify your nominal returns from your portfolio statements and overlay the latest CPI and policy guidance to estimate real returns.

In symbolic terms, if you see two assets with nominal returns R_nom1 and R_nom2 and inflation pi, your real returns are Real1 = R_nom1 − pi and Real2 = R_nom2 − pi. The one with the larger Real value is the better real performer over your chosen horizon. The exact numbers will vary with the inflation rate you observe and the asset's true nominal performance.

## Common mistakes
- - Focusing on nominal returns alone without inflation context.
- - Ignoring the horizon: short-term gains can look good, but real returns over retirement or education goals matter most.
- - Assuming high nominal returns guarantee positive real returns in a high-inflation regime.
- - Failing to account for fees and taxes that erode nominal gains before inflation is applied.
- - Not seeking pricing power or inflation resilience in the underlying businesses you own.

## Your checklist
- - Track the latest inflation reading (CPI) and compare it to your portfolio’s nominal return.
- - Calculate real returns for each holding or strategy, not just overall portfolio numbers.
- - Set a real-return target that matches your goals (e.g., preserving purchasing power over time).
- - Favor assets with pricing power or resilient cash flows that can sustain real gains when inflation ticks higher.
- - Rebalance when inflation dynamics shift or when real returns drift away from your targets.

## FAQ
- Q: What is real return?
  A: Real return is the growth in purchasing power after removing the impact of inflation; it’s roughly nominal return minus inflation.
- Q: How do I measure real return on the NSE?
  A: Use your annual nominal return on each asset or index, subtract the year’s inflation rate, and compare across assets or strategies.
- Q: Should I chase higher nominal returns to beat inflation?
  A: Not blindly. Balance nominal upside with inflation expectations, risk, and costs to aim for positive real returns over your horizon.
