# Treasury bonds for beginners - 2026-05-14

## Concept
Treasury bonds matter when you need predictable income, lower default risk, and a clearer way to judge how interest rates affect your money. In the Kenyan market, bonds are not just a place to hide. They are a benchmark for pricing risk across equities, money-market funds, and long-term portfolios.

The key idea is simple: a treasury bond pays a coupon, returns principal at maturity, and moves in price when yields move. If market yields rise after you buy, your bond becomes less attractive and its market value falls. If yields fall, the bond becomes more attractive and its price rises.

## NSE Context
For NSE investors, treasury bonds are most relevant when equity signals are thin or mixed. That was the case around this period: the market backdrop offered limited broad-based stock confirmation, while fixed-income cues remained important for judging opportunity cost. When rates stay firm, investors need stronger earnings growth or deeper valuation discounts before rotating heavily into equities.

The practical takeaway is that bond yields shape the hurdle rate for every other asset. If a government instrument can offer decent income with very low credit risk, equities must compensate you with either higher growth, better dividends, or stronger re-rating potential.

## Practical Example
Imagine two investors comparing a long-dated treasury bond with a dividend stock. The bond offers contractual income and a known maturity profile. The stock may offer better upside, but that upside depends on earnings delivery, market sentiment, and liquidity.

Now add duration risk. A short bond will usually react less to a rate move than a long bond. That means you should not treat every treasury instrument as interchangeable. If you expect rates to remain high or volatile, shorter duration often gives you more flexibility. If you expect yields to fall over time, longer duration can become more attractive because price gains may add to coupon income.

## Common Mistakes
- Chasing coupon headlines without checking how much duration risk you are taking.
- Comparing bond yields to stock returns without adjusting for credit, liquidity, and volatility.
- Locking money into long-duration paper without a clear view on rate risk and time horizon.

## Checklist
- Match bond duration to your cash-flow horizon and risk tolerance.
- Compare the yield on offer with inflation, T-bills, and dividend alternatives.
- Reassess bond allocations when CBK policy, inflation, or FX conditions change materially.
